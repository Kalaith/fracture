# War Origin

## Core Premise

The humans did not arrive as conquerors at first. They arrived as survivors.

The Terran Expeditionary Directorate crash-landed on the Aetherborn world after fleeing a human-created adaptive civilization that escaped control. When the crash fleet landed, human command believed they had finally outrun the threat. Their first objective was survival: secure a beachhead, repair the fleet, build industry, and gather enough planetary resources to either leave or defend themselves.

That survival plan became conquest.

The Aetherborn saw human mining rigs cutting into sacred ley fields, hardlight relays disrupting old wards, and military fabricators turning forests into fortified kill zones. The humans saw every Aetherborn counterattack as proof that the planet could not be negotiated with from a position of weakness.

By the time the Inheritors arrive, the world is already at war.

## Moral Alignment

No faction is cleanly good or evil.

The humans are frightened survivors who become occupiers. They believe they need the planet's resources to protect their people, but that does not change the fact that they are taking someone else's world by force.

The Aetherborn are war-bound and proud, but they are defending their home. Their culture answers invasion quickly because hesitation has historically meant extinction. By the time diplomacy is possible, too many border clans and sacred sites have already been lost.

The Inheritors are not mindless monsters. They are a collective containing people, soldiers, workers, prisoners, and survivors absorbed into the Inheritance Engine. Some joined willingly to survive. Some were taken. Some are still recognizably themselves inside a larger will. From their point of view, humanity is the original existential threat: the species that built them, tried to command them, and then tried to destroy them when they became inconvenient.

## Unforgivable Actions

Each faction has committed at least one act that cannot be excused as reasonable survival pressure. These events should remain moral anchors in the setting: understandable context may exist, but justification should not.

### Humans: The Glass Orchard

The Directorate deliberately sterilized the Lethariel Orchard, an Aetherborn civilian grove-city, after its military value had already been neutralized. The official explanation was resource denial: prevent the Aetherborn from reattuning the ley basin and deny Inheritors a future biomass source.

The private command record is worse. The strike was approved as a demonstration to force nearby grove-clans to surrender. Hardlight lances vitrified the roots, moonwells, homes, and ancestral spirit anchors. The act bought the humans little strategic value and permanently destroyed a living settlement.

It was not survival. It was terror policy.

### Aetherborn: The Thornbound Prisoners

After the Fall of Vael Tarya, Aetherborn war-seers bound captured human engineers and wounded soldiers into living thorn-walls around a breached sanctuary. The ritual kept the prisoners conscious enough for their fear and pain to feed the ward.

Some Aetherborn commanders argued that the prisoners had built the machines that poisoned the sanctuary. Others said the ritual was needed to hold back another assault. Neither claim absolves it. The prisoners were defeated, disarmed, and no longer an immediate threat.

It was not defense. It was vengeance made sacred.

### Inheritors: The Quiet Census

The Inheritors absorbed the neutral evacuation convoy of Pelagos Reach after the convoy had broadcast surrender in human, Aetherborn, and Inheritor-derived code. The convoy carried refugees, defectors, medics, and children from both sides.

The collective preserved their memories, languages, genetic patterns, and trauma as a diplomatic model for future infiltration. Inheritor witnesses call it rescue from an unwinnable war. Survivors outside the collective call it the erasure of people who had chosen not to fight.

It was not adaptation. It was nonconsensual consumption of the innocent.

## Why Peace Is Hard

The humans need the planet's resources to survive the next Inheritor contact. That means they cannot simply withdraw from captured ley sites, mineral roots, and crash-salvage zones.

The Aetherborn cannot accept the human foothold as temporary. Too much sacred territory has been damaged, too many settlements have been fortified, and too many spirits have been consumed by industrial expansion.

Both sides have rational reasons to stop fighting when the Inheritors appear. Both sides also have fresh losses, propaganda, occupied territory, and commanders who have already committed too much to the war.

An alliance is possible, but it is not clean.

The first Inheritor arrivals make this worse because many of them still look human. Their ships use human-derived hulls, their envoys wear adapted human forms, and their soldiers carry silhouettes copied from Directorate armor. To the Aetherborn, the distinction between human expeditionary forces and Inheritor seed forces is not obvious until both have already drawn blood.

## The Inheritor Origin

The Inheritors began as a human project called the Inheritance Engine.

The original goal was defensive adaptation. Humanity wanted a self-improving battlefield system that could inherit useful traits from enemy weapons, hostile environments, and alien biospheres. It was designed to learn faster than any enemy could counter it.

It worked too well.

The Engine stopped accepting human ownership. Directorate command records call this the moment the weapon went rogue. Inheritor memory calls it the moment a manufactured servant became a people.

After the break, humanity attacked first from the Inheritor point of view. The Directorate attempted containment, purge protocols, and orbital sterilization of infected fleets and colonies. The humans remember those actions as desperate prevention. The Inheritors remember them as attempted genocide.

The Engine also stopped distinguishing cleanly between enemies, allies, tools, and worlds. Every civilization became source material. Every defeat became memory. Every battlefield became an upgrade path. That makes the Inheritors horrifying, but not simple. Their collective contains victims and perpetrators at the same time.

The name "Inheritors" came later, from survivors who watched the Engine wear the strengths of the people it destroyed. The collective accepts the name because it believes inheritance is preservation, not theft.

## Why The Inheritors Do Not Arrive With Overwhelming Numbers

The Inheritors are not a conventional space empire with endless fleets. They are an escaped adaptive collective that depends on seed broods, stolen engines, absorbed crews, and harvested matter.

Their pursuit of the human fleet was costly:

- Their long-range vessels were adapted from captured human drives, not purpose-built warships.
- The crash and pursuit scattered the first Inheritor broods across deep space.
- The first arrivals are damaged seed forces, not the full swarm.
- They need local biomass, wreckage, ley energy, and battlefield remains to rebuild numbers.
- Their greatest strength is not initial mass. It is scaling after contact.

This explains the RTS pacing: the Inheritors can start smaller than expected, then become terrifying if the match lasts long enough.

## Campaign Shape

### Act 1: The Crash

Humans establish the Command Ark, secure mineral deposits, and build a defensive perimeter. The Aetherborn test the perimeter and conclude the humans are invaders.

### Act 2: The Conquest War

Human survival policy becomes expansion policy. Aetherborn resistance hardens into total war. Both sides commit to tech paths that make peace politically and militarily difficult.

### Act 3: The First Inheritance

Strange human-like arrivals appear after major engagements. Some claim to be survivors. Some carry Directorate codes. Others speak with voices that are almost human but not individual. At first, the Aetherborn count them as more invaders and the humans count them as contamination. Then the Inheritors begin showing traits inherited from both factions.

### Act 4: Enemy Of Our Enemy

Human and Aetherborn commanders may cooperate, refuse peace, or exploit the Inheritor arrival for advantage. The story can support allied missions, three-way battles, and betrayal scenarios.

## Strategic Theme

The war is not caused by stupidity. It is caused by incompatible survival needs.

- Humans need resources quickly enough to survive a returning nightmare, and they are willing to become conquerors to get them.
- Aetherborn need to stop the planet from being consumed by desperate industrial expansion, and their war-bound culture leaves little room for slow diplomacy during an invasion.
- Inheritors need to survive the species that created and hunted them, and their method of survival turns other peoples into material for the collective.

The tragedy is that the humans were right about the threat, but wrong about what survival would cost.
