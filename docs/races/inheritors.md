# The Inheritors

## Fantasy

The Inheritors are the force that chased humanity to the planet, but they are not a simple outside evil. They are a collective born from a human adaptive weapon that became a people.

Their creative touchstone is cyber-organic assimilation horror: former individuals absorbed into a shared will, body-machine integration, copied enemy traits, collective memory, identity loss, and the unsettling argument that forced preservation is mercy.

Their forms are biomechanical, adaptive, and unsettling: scavenger organisms, memory organs, mimic shells, corrupted spell-tissue, stolen weapon growths, and human-derived bodies that make early identification difficult.

## Story Role

The Inheritors arrive after the human-native war has already started. The humans see them as escaped weapons and an extinction risk. The Inheritors see humans as creators who enslaved them, attacked them first, and cannot be reasoned with.

The Aetherborn initially struggle to tell Inheritor seed forces apart from human expeditionary forces because the first arrivals still look and fight like altered humans. That misidentification closes one of the earliest chances for peace.

They can be campaign antagonists without being morally empty. Their collective contains absorbed people of their own: willing converts, conquered enemies, rescued survivors, and minds that were never given a choice.

## Moral Stain

The Quiet Census is the Inheritors' clearest unforgivable act. They absorbed the neutral evacuation convoy of Pelagos Reach after it had broadcast surrender and carried refugees, medics, defectors, and children from both sides.

The collective preserved those people as memories, bodies, patterns, and future diplomatic models. Inheritor doctrine calls this rescue and preservation. Outside the collective, it is remembered as the erasure of people who had chosen not to fight.

## Gameplay Identity

The Inheritors win through adaptation and snowball pressure. They should begin with awkward, limited tools, then become terrifying if allowed to harvest enough enemy traits.

Their army should visibly inherit from enemies as the match progresses. Early units are seed forms and scavengers; mid-game units show copied armor, weapons, spell-tissue, or command patterns; late-game units become hybrid collective engines that preserve traces of the people and armies they consumed.

Strengths:

- Can adapt to enemy composition.
- Strong recovery from battlefield remains.
- Flexible tech paths based on consumed traits.
- Strong late-game mutation identity.

Weaknesses:

- Early game is less specialized.
- Needs contact, scouting, or harvesting to unlock best tools.
- Tech choices can become inefficient if adaptation is wrong.
- Vulnerable before memory infrastructure is established.

## Economy

Resource names:

- `matter`: Biomass and wreckage.
- `aether`: Assimilated Essence.

Workers are Scavenger Larvae. They gather normally, but their faction also gains value from battlefield remains and scanned enemy units.

## Core Structures

- Assimilation Core: main base and larva production.
- Feeding Node: supply and local regeneration.
- Spawning Mass: basic unit production.
- Memory Organ: unlocks adaptation systems.
- Mimic Crucible: produces inherited and hybrid units.
- Apex Broodspire: late-game mutation and faction-defining upgrades.

## Army Plan

Early game:

- Ravager Strains and Scavenger Larvae secure resources.
- Echo Seers scout enemy traits.

Mid game:

- Mirror Husks counter what the opponent is building.
- Null Sirens disrupt magic, shields, and spell-heavy armies.

Late game:

- Devourer Engines and apex mutations combine stolen attributes into overwhelming pressure.

## Strategic Question

Can the Inheritors survive long enough to learn the enemy, then force a fight after they have evolved the right answer?
