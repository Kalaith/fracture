# Aetherborn Concord

## Fantasy

The Aetherborn Concord are the native magical civilization of the planet. They are not primitive. Their cities are grown through ritual architecture, their armies are bound to ley lines, and their commanders treat the battlefield as a living spell.

Their creative touchstone is tabletop high fantasy: magical people, wizards, enchanted beasts, summoned guardians, unicorns, spirits, ancient dragons or drakes, living forests, cursed ruins, and mythic battlefield magic. They should feel like a fantasy army brought into an RTS, not like sci-fi units with magical names.

They include elves, wizards, unicorns, sentient groves, spirit workers, beast companions, and ancient guardian magic. Their society is naturally war-bound: military duty, clan defense, and ritualized conflict are old parts of civic life, but their war in this story is defensive.

## Story Role

The Aetherborn see the human crash landing as an invasion and an ecological disaster. Human engines scar ley lines, mining rigs drain crystal fields, and hardlight generators disrupt old wards. At first, the Aetherborn objective is simple: force the humans off-world.

Their position hardens because they are attacked before there is a serious chance at peace. When Inheritor seed forces arrive in human-like forms and human-derived machines, many Aetherborn commanders count them as part of the same invasion. By the time the distinction becomes clear, the war has already widened.

Their position can change when they realize the Inheritors are not simply human reinforcements and are consuming both magic and machinery.

## Moral Stain

The Thornbound Prisoners are the Aetherborn's clearest unforgivable act. After Vael Tarya fell, captured human engineers and wounded soldiers were bound into living thorn-walls to feed a defensive ward through suffering.

It was committed by defenders of a wounded sanctuary, but the prisoners were already defeated. The act should not be explained away as ordinary planetary defense. It shows how Aetherborn war culture can turn grief, ritual, and vengeance into atrocity.

## Gameplay Identity

The Aetherborn win through map control, spell timing, mobility, and terrain manipulation. They should feel elegant and dangerous, but not brute-force efficient.

Their army should visually mix humanoid casters and warriors with magical creatures. A baseline force might include elven wardens, wizard adepts, unicorn lancers, treant-like sentinels, fae scouts, summoned elementals, gryphon or drake riders, and late-game archmage or elder-beast centerpieces.

Strengths:

- Strong control around ley nodes.
- Excellent spellcasters and area denial.
- Fast harassment through unicorn and spirit units.
- Strong defensive positions once groves are established.

Weaknesses:

- Fragile economy if gatherers are caught away from wards.
- Expensive advanced units.
- Needs map awareness to get full value from spells.
- Poor at replacing large armies quickly.

## Economy

Resource names:

- `matter`: Verdant Mass, gathered from living groves and mineral roots.
- `aether`: Ley Essence, gathered from ley crystals and moonwells.

Workers are Sprite Gatherers. They are fast and fragile. Aetherborn buildings are grown by workers and are strongest inside Grove Influence.

## Core Structures

- Heartwood Nexus: main base and worker production.
- Moonwell: supply and local recovery.
- Grove Circle: basic military production.
- Arcane Spire: spellcaster tech.
- Unicorn Glade: mobility and support cavalry.
- Elder Sanctum: late-game magic and global ritual upgrades.

## Army Plan

Early game:

- Sprite Gatherers build economy.
- Elven Wardens defend and scout.
- Grove Sentinels hold ley nodes.

Mid game:

- Wizard Adepts unlock disruption.
- Unicorn Lancers create mobility and healing windows.

Late game:

- Archmage Avatars create decisive spell moments.
- Elder rituals reinforce defensive terrain and punish clustered armies.

## Strategic Question

Can the Aetherborn control the map long enough for their spells and elite units to matter before the humans out-produce them or the Inheritors adapt?
