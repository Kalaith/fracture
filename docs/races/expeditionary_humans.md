# Terran Expeditionary Directorate

## Fantasy

The Terran Expeditionary Directorate is a crash-landed human military-industrial expedition. Their designs are sleek, precise, and engineered around survival: hardlight panels, clean drones, modular walkers, rail weapons, powered armor, drop infrastructure, and deployable fabrication.

Their creative touchstone is militarized expeditionary sci-fi: disciplined infantry, aggressive fleet doctrine, patriotic command culture, armored landing zones, powered suits, mobile firebases, combat engineers, and a society that can justify occupation as defense.

They are not here because they wanted conquest. They ran from the Inheritors and crashed into a world that does not want them. Their tragedy is that fear turns a survival mission into occupation.

## Story Role

The humans initially hide the full truth of the Inheritor threat. To the Aetherborn, that makes every generator and mining rig look like hostile occupation. To the humans, every delay risks extinction.

The Directorate also carries guilt it cannot admit. The Inheritors came from human research, and some Inheritor memories insist humanity attacked first when the collective became self-aware. Directorate command rejects that framing, but the claim is not baseless.

Their story can branch toward alliance, evacuation, conquest, or desperate last stand.

## Moral Stain

The Glass Orchard is the Directorate's clearest unforgivable act. Human command sterilized the Lethariel Orchard after the site had stopped being a meaningful military threat, using hardlight lances to destroy a living Aetherborn civilian settlement and its ancestral spirit anchors.

The strike was framed as resource denial and future Inheritor prevention, but private records show it was also meant to terrify nearby grove-clans into surrender. It was not survival pressure. It was occupation policy turned into terror.

## Gameplay Identity

The humans win through efficient build orders, production scaling, ranged firepower, and tech timing. They should feel crisp and industrial, with fewer surprises than the Aetherborn but stronger execution.

Their army should look like a professional expeditionary war machine rather than sleek alien mysticism: rifle squads, powered infantry, mobile artillery, combat drones, walkers, dropship support, bunker lines, and hardline command assets.

Strengths:

- Strong production and replacement rate.
- Good ranged combat and siege tools.
- Reliable scouting through drones.
- Strong upgrades that reward clean infrastructure.

Weaknesses:

- Dependent on power relays and production buildings.
- Less flexible when caught out of position.
- Aether conversion is slower than native magical gathering.
- Vulnerable to disruption of expansions and power grids.

## Economy

Resource names:

- `matter`: Salvage and planetary minerals.
- `aether`: Converted Aether, refined through human converters.

Workers are Field Engineers. Human structures are constructed quickly but require power coverage for advanced functions.

## Core Structures

- Command Ark: main base and worker production.
- Supply Pylon: supply and local logistics.
- Fabricator Bay: infantry and basic vehicles.
- Power Relay: building enablement and expansion support.
- Drone Foundry: scouting, air, and support production.
- Hardlight Lab: advanced shields, walkers, and upgrades.
- Orbital Uplink: late-game global abilities.

## Army Plan

Early game:

- Ranger Troopers and Pulse Bikes defend, scout, and raid.
- Field Engineers expand quickly if protected.

Mid game:

- Aegis Walkers anchor the army.
- Valkyrie Dronewings and Rail Artillery add pressure.

Late game:

- Titan Frames and orbital support create decisive pushes.

## Strategic Question

Can the humans build enough infrastructure to survive the native war and prepare for the Inheritors before their temporary foothold collapses?
